## LED Spotlight Product Development

As a Product Development Engineering Intern at Methode Electronics, I worked on the redesign and validation of a high-intensity LED vehicle spotlight.

My work centered on redesigning the mechanical and thermal architecture, developing production-oriented CAD, supporting prototype manufacturing, debugging hardware during bring-up, and validating the resulting system through thermal and photometric testing.

### Current Results

- redesigned the housing around an integrated aluminum structural and thermal architecture
- reduced assembly mass while improving heat rejection
- achieved approximately **46% lower PCB operating temperature** during prototype testing
- resolved unstable LED operation during prototype bring-up
- demonstrated greater than **200,000 cd** peak optical intensity
- supported the design from CAD through prototype sourcing and physical validation

## What I Owned

My work included:

- CATIA V5 mechanical design and packaging
- housing and thermal-path architecture development
- ribbing, interfaces, fastener access, and production-oriented DFM
- thermal-interface and coating considerations
- prototype supplier coordination
- thermal testing and design iteration
- LED driver troubleshooting during hardware bring-up
- photometric testing and beam-performance evaluation
- integration of mechanical, electrical, optical, and manufacturing requirements

## Mechanical & Thermal Redesign

The existing product used separate components to provide the enclosure and primary heat-rejection functions.

I redesigned the architecture around an aluminum housing that could perform both roles, creating a more direct thermal path from the LED assembly into the exterior structure while reducing mass and component complexity.

The housing was developed with production manufacturability in mind rather than as a purely thermal geometry. The design therefore had to balance heat transfer, structural stiffness, casting-oriented geometry, external radius requirements, coating, assembly access, and surrounding optical and electrical hardware.

## Prototype Bring-Up and Validation

The project continued beyond CAD into prototype hardware.

During bring-up, I investigated unstable LED output and traced the issue into the driver current-setting circuit. After correcting the configuration, the prototype operated stably and could be evaluated through thermal and photometric testing.

The resulting testing showed a substantial reduction in PCB operating temperature while maintaining the high optical intensity required of the spotlight.

## Engineering Outcome

The project gave me ownership across much of the product-development cycle:

**architecture → CAD → DFM → prototype → debug → test → iteration**

It also required treating the spotlight as a coupled mechanical, thermal, electrical, optical, and manufacturing system rather than optimizing any one discipline in isolation.
